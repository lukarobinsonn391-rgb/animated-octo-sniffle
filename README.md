<h1><strong>How Strict Is Greyhound About Baggage? What Actually Happens If You're Over the Limit</strong></h1>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Short answer: stricter than the relaxed, "just toss your bag under the bus" reputation bus travel used to have.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Greyhound weighs bags, measures them, and charges real fees for going over, and agents at the counter generally don't waive those fees, according to current baggage guides and traveler reports.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The good news is the rules are consistent and predictable once you know them, so a little prep before you head to the station avoids almost every surprise fee.</span></p>
<h2><strong>The Basic Allowance</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Greyhound's standard policy, as described across current baggage guides, gives you two pieces for free:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>One carry-on bag</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">, up to 25 lbs (11 kg), that must fit under your seat or in the overhead bin, generally within around 16 x 12 x 7 inches</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>One checked bag</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">, up to 50 lbs (23 kg) and around 62 inches total when you add length, width, and height (roughly 30 x 20 x 12 inches), stored in the compartment under the bus</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Strollers and car seats typically don't count against this allowance and travel free, which matters if you're navigating the system with young kids.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Yes, Greyhound Actually Weighs and Measures Bags</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is the part that surprises people who remember bus travel as informal.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Multiple 2026 baggage guides confirm Greyhound weighs and measures bags at check-in, and the scales at the terminal are calibrated, meaning agents aren't eyeballing it.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">One guide specifically notes agents "will not hesitate to charge for even a few extra pounds," and recommends weighing your bag at home before you leave so there are no surprises at the counter.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Why the strictness? Greyhound's under-bus storage space is shared with Greyhound Package Express, the company's commercial freight and shipping service, according to current travel guidance.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That shared space is part of why weight and size limits get enforced more consistently than the "loose bus travel" reputation might suggest.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">There's genuinely limited room, and it's being split between passenger bags and paid freight.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This focus on managing passenger luggage also fits within broader federal bus-safety requirements, with the </span><a href="https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/safety/passenger-safety/passenger-carrier-safety-information"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's passenger carrier safety information</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> covering safety requirements and resources for commercial bus operators.</span></p>
<h2><strong>What Does It Actually Cost to Go Over?&nbsp;</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Here's where the numbers get specific, and where you should budget accordingly if you know you're traveling heavy:</span></p>
<p><strong>Excess baggage (a second or third bag):</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Fees generally range from about $21.99 to $44.99 per additional bag, according to current baggage-fee tracking.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You're typically permitted up to two extra bags beyond your free allowance, for a maximum of three checked bags total, though this can vary by route.</span></p>
<p><strong>Overweight bags:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> If your checked bag exceeds 50 lbs, expect a fee that scales with both the excess weight and the distance traveled.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">One breakdown of Greyhound's overweight structure lists roughly $20 for a bag between 50 and 75 lbs on trips up to 200 miles, $25 for the same weight range between 201 and 800 miles, and $30 beyond that.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Other current sources cite a broader range of $21.99 to $44.99 depending on how far over the limit you are, so exact pricing appears to vary by route and by how Greyhound's current dynamic baggage pricing responds to your specific booking.</span></p>
<p><strong>Bulky items:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> A single oversized item, up to about 75 to 95 lbs depending on the source, is generally allowed with a fee applied, since it still has to fit in the shared under-bus compartment.</span></p>
<p><strong>Truly oversized items:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> If a bag doesn't fit the available space at all, current guidance says it may need to be sent separately through Greyhound Package Express rather than traveling with you on the same bus.</span></p>
<h2><strong>What Happens If You Don't Pay or Can't Comply?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is where "strict" becomes a real, practical concern rather than just a policy detail.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">According to current guidance on Greyhound's baggage enforcement, if your bag is oversized or overweight, you generally have three outcomes:</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Pay the fee and check the bag.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> This is by far the most common resolution, and it's what most travelers end up doing.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Repack on the spot.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Moving items into your personal item or wearing extra layers of clothing can sometimes bring a bag back under the limit, avoiding the fee entirely.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Miss your bus.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> If a bag is significantly over the limit and you're unwilling or unable to pay, guidance on Greyhound's enforcement notes you could be denied boarding with that bag, or in a worst case, denied boarding altogether if you can't resolve the situation before departure.</span></li>
</ol>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That third outcome is rare, but it underscores the core point: Greyhound's baggage rules aren't just guidelines that get waved through at a busy counter.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Agents are checking, and the fee structure exists specifically because space is limited and shared with freight.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Items Greyhound Restricts or Prohibits</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Beyond size and weight, Greyhound maintains rules about what can travel in your bags at all, similar in spirit to airline restrictions, though generally less exhaustive.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For additional context on bus travel safety, the </span><a href="https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/safety/passenger-safety/bus-passenger-safety-tips"><span style="font-weight: 400;">FMCSA's bus passenger safety tips</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> explain what travelers should know before boarding, including safety requirements and how to report safety or service issues.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Current guidance notes that weapons, flammable items, and certain other restricted goods are not permitted, and it's worth checking Greyhound's official list directly.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you're traveling with anything unusual, tools, sporting equipment, or specialty items, it's worth checking the rules in advance, since enforcement of these categories tends to be stricter than the general weight and size rules.</span></p>
<h2><strong>How to Avoid Baggage Fees Entirely?</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A little preparation goes a long way here, since almost every fee in this article is avoidable with basic planning:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Weigh your bags at home</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> using a household or luggage scale before you leave, rather than finding out at the terminal.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Measure, don't estimate.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Include handles and wheels when you check your bag's total dimensions; a bag that looks fine can tip over the 62-inch combined limit once you measure it properly.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Consolidate into fewer, well-packed bags</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> rather than several smaller ones, since the fee structure penalizes bag count as much as total weight.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Use your personal item strategically.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> A travel pillow, jacket, or small blanket in your carry-on can free up space in your checked bag without technically adding a new item.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Arrive early</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">, particularly if you're checking a bag, since resolving a weight or size issue at the counter takes time you don't want to be rushing through before departure.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Check current fees and rules for your specific route before you travel</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">, since Greyhound's baggage pricing is now dynamic and can shift based on when and how you book.</span></li>
</ul>
